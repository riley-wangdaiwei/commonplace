# Defaults and the having-to-explain friction

## Question

Is part of the cost of opting out of a default the cost of having to explain yourself — i.e., exiting the shared common ground that the default provides?

## Why it matters

The consent-architecture cost model (professor's frame: information acquisition vs. toggle-switching) may be missing a third cost: leaving a default means leaving common ground, and the system taxes the leaver in explanations. "Are you sure?" opt-out dialogs are the platform demanding an explanation from the non-default choice. The default user is legible without explaining; the opt-out user must justify herself to keep going. Paragraph for the narrative section on defaults and costly attention.

## Notes

- Her example (2026-10-18): same school as a kid — "the purple book, p.151" needs zero explanation because the default (same classes, schedule, teachers) is shared. As adults meeting after five years apart, every reference needs grounding work first: why I'm doing this, why now, why these people. Defaults = institutionalized common ground: legal defaults (forms, documents) and cultural defaults decide who never has to explain.
- Theory anchors:
  - **Herbert Clark — common ground** (*Using Language*, 1996): conversation as joint action; shared assumptions make reference cheap. The school example is literally this.
  - **Harold Garfinkel — breaching experiments** (*Studies in Ethnomethodology*, 1967): subjects instructed to act as if common ground didn't exist produced exactly "having to explain everything," met with bewilderment and anger. Direct empirical demo of the friction.
  - **Bourdieu — doxa** (*Outline of a Theory of Practice*, 1977): the taken-for-granted that "goes without saying"; doxa never explains, only heterodoxy must argue. The philosophical version of defaults.
  - **James Scott — legibility** (*Seeing Like a State*, 1998): the default position is the legible one; non-defaults must render themselves legible — i.e., explain.
  - **Thaler & Sunstein — choice architecture** (*Nudge*, 2008); **Johnson, Bellman & Lohse (2002)** on privacy defaults: the econ baseline for opt-in/opt-out stickiness (already in reading list 7.0).
  - **Coase/Williamson — transaction costs**: explanation as the transaction cost of deviating from the institutional default; the econ-language version of the same claim.
