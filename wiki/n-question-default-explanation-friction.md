# Defaults and the having-to-explain friction

## Question

Is part of the cost of opting out of a default the cost of having to explain yourself — i.e., exiting the shared common ground that the default provides?

## Why it matters

The consent-architecture cost model (professor's frame: information acquisition vs. toggle-switching) may be missing a third cost: leaving a default means leaving common ground, and the system taxes the leaver in explanations. "Are you sure?" opt-out dialogs are the platform demanding an explanation from the non-default choice. Connects to the narrative section on defaults and costly attention.

## Notes

- Her example (2026-10-18): same school as a kid — "the purple book, p.151" needs zero explanation because the default (same classes, schedule, teachers) is shared. As adults, every reference needs grounding work first. Defaults = institutionalized common ground: legal defaults and cultural defaults decide who never has to explain.
